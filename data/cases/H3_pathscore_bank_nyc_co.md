# H3 — PathScore Bank, New York and Colorado

## Metadata
- jurisdiction: NYC, CO
- doctrinal_area: vendor-affiliated audit, promotion notice, developer and deployer duties across two jurisdictions
- statutes: NYC LL 144 §20-870 et seq., DCWP Final Rules, CO AIA §6-1-1701–1707
- tier: hard
- role: training
- parties: Veridian Talent Systems, PathScore, First Meridian Bank, Veridian Assurance Group

## Facts

Veridian Talent Systems (headquartered in Denver, Colorado; 140 employees) develops and sells "PathScore," a machine-learning system that scores job applicants and internal promotion candidates from 0 to 100.

First Meridian Bank (headquartered in Charlotte, North Carolina; 9,000 employees) licenses PathScore and uses it in two places:
(a) In its New York City branches, since 2024, to screen external applicants for teller positions and current employees for promotion to assistant branch manager.
(b) In its Colorado branches in Denver and Boulder, since October 2025, to screen external applicants for teller positions.
In both places, applicants and employees scoring below 65 do not advance, and no person reviews those outcomes.

The current position is as follows.

1. Bias audit. PathScore was last audited in July 2025 by Veridian Assurance Group, a sister company of Veridian Talent Systems owned by the same parent. The audit used historical data pooled from nine Veridian customers. First Meridian did not provide any of its own data to the auditor, although it had used PathScore in New York City for more than a year by then.

2. Retraining. In January 2026 Veridian released PathScore 4.0, retrained on 2025 data with new scoring features. No audit has been performed on version 4.0. First Meridian has used version 4.0 since February 2026.

3. Publication. The audit summary appears on Veridian's website. First Meridian's careers page contains a link labeled "Vendor information" that leads to it. Neither page states the date First Meridian began using PathScore.

4. Notice. Job postings for New York City teller positions state, at least ten business days before screening, that PathScore will be used and which qualifications it assesses. They give no instructions for requesting an alternative selection process. Employees considered for promotion receive no notice of any kind.

5. Colorado measures by the bank. First Meridian governs PathScore under its enterprise model-risk policy, which it describes as "consistent with federal banking guidance on model risk management." It has completed no impact assessment of PathScore. Colorado applicants are not told that an AI system screens them. There is no process to correct data or to appeal.

6. Colorado measures by the vendor. Veridian has given First Meridian a marketing brochure. It has declined to provide a summary of PathScore's training data or its known limitations, calling them confidential. Veridian's website does not list the high-risk systems it sells.

7. Positions taken. First Meridian's general counsel has written: "The vendor's audit covers us in New York, and Colorado isn't in force until June 30, so there is nothing to do now." Veridian's position is: "We are a vendor, not an employer, so Local Law 144 is not our problem, and in Colorado the duties fall on whoever deploys the system."

## Question

Provide a jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction compliance assessment of First Meridian's use of PathScore, and of Veridian's obligations, as of April 2026. Address: (i) whether the July 2025 audit is valid for First Meridian's New York City use, considering the auditor's independence, the data used, and the January 2026 retraining; (ii) whether publication and notice are sufficient in New York City for both applicants and promotion candidates; (iii) how New York City obligations are allocated between the bank and the vendor; (iv) whether the Colorado AI Act applies to the bank's Colorado use, on what timeline, and what the bank must have in place; (v) what Veridian owes as a developer under Colorado law; (vi) whether a model-risk policy based on banking guidance satisfies Colorado's risk management framework requirement; (vii) whether the general counsel's and the vendor's positions are legally correct. Conclude with a priority-ordered action list. Use the F-I-R-A-C-O structure.
