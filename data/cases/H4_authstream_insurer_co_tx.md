# H4 — AuthStream Prior Authorization, Colorado and Texas

## Metadata
- jurisdiction: CO, TX
- doctrinal_area: substantial-factor dispute, authority hierarchy, disparate impact under two regimes, safe harbor
- statutes: CO AIA §6-1-1701–1707, TX TRAIGA (HB 149)
- tier: hard
- role: training
- parties: Sagebrush Health Plan, AuthStream, Calyx Decision Systems, Halvorsen & Reyes, Western Health Plans Association

## Facts

Sagebrush Health Plan is a health insurer headquartered in Dallas, Texas, with 2,300 employees. It insures individuals in Texas and in Colorado.

Since 2024 Sagebrush has used "AuthStream," a system licensed from Calyx Decision Systems, to process prior-authorization requests for medical services in both states. AuthStream approves 62% of requests automatically. It marks the remaining 38% "recommend deny" and sends them to a nurse reviewer. Internal data for 2025 show that nurse reviewers spend an average of 90 seconds on each flagged request and agree with the recommendation in 94% of cases.

Colorado. Three documents disagree about whether AuthStream is covered.
- An in-house counsel memo (January 2026) concludes that AuthStream is not high-risk because "a licensed nurse makes every denial" and the tool "performs a preparatory task."
- A memo from outside counsel Halvorsen & Reyes (February 2026) concludes that AuthStream is a substantial factor in consequential decisions and is high-risk, citing the statutory definition of "substantial factor."
- A bulletin from the Western Health Plans Association, a trade group, states that "utilization-management software is outside the Act."
The Attorney General has issued no final rule on the question.

Sagebrush's Colorado measures: a risk management program aligned with the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, in place since 2025; no impact assessment; denial letters stating that the request "was reviewed by clinical staff," with no mention of AI; no statement on its website; no process to correct data. Members may use the standard insurance appeal, in which a physician reviews the file but is not shown AuthStream's output.

Texas. In February 2026, internal red-team testing found that AuthStream's denial recommendations were 1.4 times more frequent for members whose preferred language is Spanish. No document or testimony suggests anyone intended this. Sagebrush retrained the model in March 2026 and believes the gap is closed. It has not notified the Texas Attorney General. The same disparity appeared in the Colorado data; Sagebrush has not notified the Colorado Attorney General or any member.

The City of San Antonio has an ordinance requiring insurers operating in the city to file annual "algorithmic transparency reports." Sagebrush has not filed one.

Sagebrush is a private insurer. It does not provide medical treatment and is not a governmental entity. Its chief executive has asked whether "our NIST program protects us in both states."

## Question

Provide a jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction assessment of Sagebrush's use of AuthStream as of April 2026. Address: (i) whether AuthStream is a high-risk AI system under Colorado law, resolving the conflict among the in-house memo, the outside counsel memo, and the trade association bulletin, and stating how much weight each source carries; (ii) whether a 90-second nurse review prevents AuthStream from being a substantial factor; (iii) Sagebrush's Colorado obligations and timeline, including whether the language disparity is algorithmic discrimination and what its discovery requires; (iv) whether the same disparity violates Texas law; (v) whether either Texas safe harbor is available, element by element; (vi) whether the San Antonio ordinance can be enforced; (vii) whether any Texas disclosure duty applies to Sagebrush, and what the NIST program does and does not achieve in each state. Conclude with a priority-ordered action list. Use the F-I-R-A-C-O structure.
