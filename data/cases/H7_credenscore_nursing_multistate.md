# H7 — CredenScore Nurse Staffing, Three States

## Metadata
- jurisdiction: NYC, CO, TX
- doctrinal_area: multi-jurisdictional hiring tool, stale audit and promotion notice, framework mismatch, Texas government and healthcare disclosure
- statutes: NYC LL 144 §20-870 et seq., DCWP Final Rules, CO AIA §6-1-1701–1707, TX TRAIGA (HB 149)
- tier: hard
- role: test
- parties: ShiftBridge Health, CredenScore, Empire Mercy, Rocky Vista Health, Bexar County Hospital District, Tolland Review Partners, AskBexar

## Facts

ShiftBridge Health (headquartered in Chicago, Illinois; 450 employees) develops "CredenScore," a system that scores nurses applying for staff positions at hospitals. ShiftBridge licenses CredenScore to hospital systems, including three relevant here.

Empire Mercy operates hospitals in New York City. Its hiring rubric gives the CredenScore result 50% of the total weight, more than any other criterion. Empire Mercy uses CredenScore both to hire nurses and to select current nurses for promotion to charge nurse.

Rocky Vista Health operates hospitals in Colorado and has 3,000 full-time employees. It has used CredenScore since 2025. Applicants scoring below a cutoff are rejected automatically.

Bexar County Hospital District is a public hospital district in Texas, a governmental entity. It uses CredenScore to shortlist nurse applicants.

1. New York City audit. CredenScore was audited in February 2025 by Tolland Review Partners, a firm with no financial or employment ties to ShiftBridge or the hospitals and no role in building the tool. The audit used historical data pooled from several hospitals; Empire Mercy contributed its own data. No audit has been done since.

2. New York City publication and notice. Empire Mercy posts the audit summary and distribution date in a clearly labeled section of its careers page. External applicants are told, at least ten business days before screening, that CredenScore will be used and which qualifications it assesses, and how to request an alternative process. Nurses considered for promotion receive no notice.

3. Colorado, the hospital. Rocky Vista describes its AI governance as "our HIPAA security program plus ISO/IEC 27001 certification." Its only impact assessment is a generic document written by ShiftBridge that does not describe Rocky Vista's deployment. Applicants are not told an AI system screens them. There is no appeal and no way to correct data.

4. Colorado, the developer. ShiftBridge provides hospitals with documentation of CredenScore's known limitations and a summary of its training data. It provides nothing on intended or inappropriate uses and nothing on how staff should use or monitor the scores. Its website does not list its high-risk systems.

5. Texas. Bexar County Hospital District tells nurse applicants nothing about CredenScore. Separately, it runs "AskBexar," an AI chatbot that answers questions from patients and the public on its website, without stating that it is an AI system. It also uses an AI discharge-planning tool in patient care and does not tell patients. Nothing suggests any intent to discriminate or cause harm.

6. ShiftBridge's position. ShiftBridge tells customers: "One compliance package satisfies all three states. Our audit and our impact assessment travel with the product."

## Question

Provide a jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction compliance assessment as of April 2026, covering ShiftBridge and each of the three hospital systems. Address: (i) whether CredenScore is an automated employment decision tool at Empire Mercy, and whether the February 2025 audit is current; (ii) whether Empire Mercy's publication and notice are sufficient for both applicants and promotion candidates; (iii) whether the Colorado AI Act applies to Rocky Vista, on what timeline, and whether its governance framework and vendor-written impact assessment are sufficient; (iv) what ShiftBridge owes as a developer under Colorado law and what is missing; (v) whether Texas law requires anything of Bexar County Hospital District in its hiring use of CredenScore; (vi) whether the AskBexar chatbot and the discharge-planning tool create Texas disclosure obligations, and under which provisions; (vii) whether ShiftBridge's "one compliance package" claim is legally correct. Conclude with a priority-ordered action list. Use the F-I-R-A-C-O structure.
