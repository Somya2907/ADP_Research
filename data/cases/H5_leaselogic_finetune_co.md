# H5 — LeaseLogic Custom Model

## Metadata
- jurisdiction: CO
- doctrinal_area: small-deployer exception lost by own-data training, deployer becoming developer, authority hierarchy, discovery and cure
- statutes: CO AIA (SB 24-205) §6-1-1701 through §6-1-1707
- tier: hard
- role: training
- parties: Aspen Ridge Living, LeaseLogic, Corbel AI

## Facts

Aspen Ridge Living manages 2,600 rental units in Grand Junction and Montrose, Colorado. It has 46 full-time employees and about 30 part-time and seasonal workers.

Since 2024 Aspen Ridge has used "LeaseLogic," a tenant-screening system sold by Corbel AI, to approve or deny rental applications and to set security-deposit amounts. Leasing agents may override the system. In 2025 they did so in 3% of applications.

Until late 2025 Aspen Ridge used LeaseLogic exactly as Corbel shipped it. In November 2025 Aspen Ridge sent Corbel six years of its own tenant payment and eviction records. Corbel used them to produce a customized version, "LeaseLogic-AR." Aspen Ridge's own analyst selected which data fields the customized model would use and set its approval thresholds. Aspen Ridge has used LeaseLogic-AR since December 2025.

Three sources give Aspen Ridge conflicting advice.
- An email from Corbel's account manager: "Small landlords are exempt, so you don't need to do anything."
- A memo from Aspen Ridge's own attorney (January 2026): the small-deployer exception was lost when Aspen Ridge's data was used for training, and by directing the customization Aspen Ridge may itself have become a developer.
- A published FAQ from the state apartment association: "Count only full-time employees. Under fifty, and you are exempt from the Act entirely."

In February 2026 an internal review found that LeaseLogic-AR denied applicants whose income came mainly from disability benefits at twice the rate of other applicants with comparable income and rental history. In March 2026 Aspen Ridge changed the model's thresholds and believes the gap has narrowed. It notified no one.

Aspen Ridge's current measures:
- The rental application states: "Automated screening may be used."
- There is no risk management policy, no impact assessment, and no statement on the website.
- Denied applicants receive a form letter that does not mention AI. There is no process to correct data or to appeal.
- No review of the system for discriminatory outcomes was performed before February 2026.

Corbel's measures:
- Corbel gave Aspen Ridge full documentation for the standard LeaseLogic product: limitations, training data summary, intended uses.
- Corbel has provided no documentation for LeaseLogic-AR.
- Corbel's public website lists LeaseLogic among its high-risk systems but does not mention customized versions.

Aspen Ridge's owner prefers the apartment association's reading and wants to know whether she can rely on it.

## Question

Assess the position of Aspen Ridge and of Corbel under the Colorado AI Act (SB 24-205) as of the expected effective date (June 30, 2026). Address: (i) whether LeaseLogic-AR is a high-risk AI system and a substantial factor, given the override rate; (ii) whether the small-deployer exception applies, analyzing the employee count, the own-data condition, and the scope of the exception even where it applies; (iii) whether Aspen Ridge is a developer of LeaseLogic-AR, and what Aspen Ridge and Corbel each owe as developers; (iv) how much weight the account manager's email, the attorney's memo, and the apartment association's FAQ each carry, and which reading is correct; (v) whether the February 2026 finding is algorithmic discrimination, and what its discovery requires and by when; (vi) whether the rebuttable presumption of reasonable care is available; (vii) the full list of obligations for each party with deadlines, including the impact assessment timing for a system deployed and modified before the effective date. Conclude with a priority-ordered action list. Use the F-I-R-A-C-O structure.
