# H6 — RankRight Staffing, New York and Texas

## Metadata
- jurisdiction: NYC, TX
- doctrinal_area: out-of-state employment agency, preemption argument, auditor independence and test data, intentional discrimination
- statutes: NYC LL 144 §20-870 et seq., DCWP Final Rules, TX TRAIGA (HB 149)
- tier: hard
- role: training
- parties: Caprock Staffing Group, RankRight, Dunmore AI, Ellery Insight, Baystate Retail

## Facts

Caprock Staffing Group is an employment agency headquartered in Dallas, Texas, with 1,200 employees. It screens candidates on behalf of client employers. About 70% of the positions it fills are in Texas. About 30% are positions in New York City or remote positions for which candidates living in New York City apply; most of these are for its client Baystate Retail.

Caprock uses "RankRight," a tool licensed from Dunmore AI, to score every candidate. For the first cut, recruiters rely on the RankRight score alone: candidates below the cutoff are removed and no other factor is considered.

1. Bias audit. RankRight was audited in November 2025 by Ellery Insight. Ellery's lead auditor worked at Dunmore AI as a data scientist on RankRight until mid-2024. The audit used test data generated by Ellery rather than historical data, although Caprock has three years of its own historical data from using RankRight. The summary of results does not explain why historical data was not used or how the test data was produced.

2. Publication. Caprock posted the audit summary and RankRight's distribution date in a clearly labeled section of its website.

3. Notice. Candidates receive an email on the day they are screened. It says an automated tool will be used and lists the qualifications assessed. It does not explain how to request an alternative selection process.

4. Volume. Between December 2025 and March 2026, RankRight screened 2,900 candidates who live in New York City.

5. Caprock's legal position. Its general counsel has written: "TRAIGA preempts local AI regulation, so New York City's Local Law 144 cannot be applied to a Texas company. In any event we are an agency. The client is the employer, so any obligation is the client's."

6. Texas practices. Caprock does not tell Texas candidates that an AI tool screens them. In January 2026 a recruiting team lead instructed analysts by email to lower RankRight scores for candidates from six named ZIP codes, writing: "the client doesn't want people from those neighborhoods — you know which ones." Residents of those ZIP codes are predominantly Black and Hispanic. The adjustment was applied for nine weeks.

7. Dallas ordinance. A 2025 City of Dallas ordinance requires employers and staffing firms to register any hiring algorithm with the city. Caprock has not registered RankRight.

8. Governance. Caprock has no AI governance program and has not adopted the NIST AI Risk Management Framework. It has not received any notice from the Texas Attorney General or from the NYC Department of Consumer and Worker Protection.

## Question

Provide a jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction compliance assessment of Caprock's use of RankRight as of April 2026. Address: (i) whether Local Law 144 applies to a Texas-headquartered employment agency, including for remote positions; (ii) whether TRAIGA preempts Local Law 144 and whether the "client is the employer" argument succeeds; (iii) whether the November 2025 audit is valid, considering the auditor's history and the use of test data; (iv) whether notice is sufficient, and Caprock's penalty exposure in New York City; (v) whether the ZIP-code instruction violates TRAIGA, and whether RankRight's use without that instruction would; (vi) whether the Dallas ordinance can be enforced, and whether Caprock owes Texas candidates any AI disclosure; (vii) how enforcement and cure would operate in each jurisdiction. Conclude with a priority-ordered action list. Use the F-I-R-A-C-O structure.
