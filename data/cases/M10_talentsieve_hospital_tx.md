# M10 — TalentSieve and Texas Intent

## Metadata
- jurisdiction: TX
- doctrinal_area: intentional discrimination versus disparate impact, local preemption, safe harbors
- statutes: TX TRAIGA (HB 149) §551.001 et seq.
- tier: medium
- role: test
- parties: Gulfstream Regional Medical, TalentSieve, Pecos Analytics, DischargePredict

## Facts

Gulfstream Regional Medical is a private hospital system in Houston, Texas, with 6,000 employees.

For hiring, Gulfstream uses "TalentSieve," a résumé-screening tool licensed from Pecos Analytics. A 2025 internal analysis found that TalentSieve advances women at 0.71 times the rate of men. Nothing in the tool's design documents or in Gulfstream's records suggests that anyone intended that result.

In one department, however, a hiring manager configured TalentSieve to exclude every applicant whose graduation year was before 1995. In an email to the recruiting team the manager wrote: "I want a young team — screen out the over-50s."

Gulfstream does not tell job applicants that an AI tool screens their applications.

In 2024 the City of Houston adopted an ordinance requiring employers in the city to obtain an annual bias audit of any AI hiring tool. In February 2026 the city sent Gulfstream a notice demanding an audit of TalentSieve.

For patient care, Gulfstream uses "DischargePredict," an AI system that helps plan the timing of each patient's discharge. Every patient is told that the system is being used, in writing, on the admission form signed on the day care begins.

Since 2025 Gulfstream has maintained a documented AI governance program built on the NIST AI Risk Management Framework. It found the hiring manager's filter itself, during an internal review in March 2026, and removed it the same week. It did not notify the Attorney General. On March 30, 2026, the Texas Attorney General sent Gulfstream a written notice of an alleged violation concerning the filter.

## Question

Assess Gulfstream's compliance position under applicable US AI law as of April 2026. Specifically address: (1) Whether TalentSieve's lower advancement rate for women violates TRAIGA; (2) Whether the hiring manager's filter violates TRAIGA; (3) Whether the Houston ordinance can be enforced against Gulfstream; (4) Whether Gulfstream owes any AI disclosure to job applicants, and whether its patient disclosure is sufficient; (5) Who may enforce TRAIGA, the penalties, how the cure period works, and whether either safe harbor is available on these facts. Use the F-I-R-A-C-O structure.
