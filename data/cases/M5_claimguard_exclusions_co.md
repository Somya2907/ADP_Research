# M5 — ClaimGuard and the High-Risk Exclusions

## Metadata
- jurisdiction: CO
- doctrinal_area: high-risk exclusions, substantial factor, risk management framework
- statutes: CO AIA (SB 24-205) §6-1-1701 through §6-1-1707
- tier: medium
- role: training
- parties: Cimarron Peak Insurance, DocParse, PatternWatch, ClaimGuard, Graywolf Analytics

## Facts

Cimarron Peak Insurance is a homeowners insurer in Colorado Springs, Colorado, with 420 full-time employees. It uses three AI tools supplied by Graywolf Analytics.

"DocParse" reads submitted claim forms and copies names, dates, and amounts into the claims system. It makes no evaluation of a claim.

"PatternWatch" runs once a quarter on claims that are already closed. It flags adjusters whose payout patterns differ from their past patterns and sends a report to managers. It has no effect on any individual claim.

"ClaimGuard" assigns a fraud score from 0 to 100 to every open claim. Claims scoring above 80 are routed to "special handling." In 2025, 81% of claims in special handling were denied, and 37% of those policyholders were later non-renewed. Adjusters may override the routing, and did so in 6% of cases.

Cimarron Peak's compliance position is:
- One "AI governance policy" covers all three tools. It is certified to ISO/IEC 27001, an information-security standard.
- An impact assessment was completed for DocParse only.
- The website states: "We use technology to process claims efficiently."
- Denial and non-renewal letters do not mention any AI system.
- A policyholder may use the general claims-appeal process, in which a second adjuster reviews the file. That adjuster is not shown the ClaimGuard score.
- There is no process for correcting data that ClaimGuard used.

Cimarron Peak's general counsel has concluded that none of the three tools is high-risk: DocParse is clerical, PatternWatch only detects patterns, and ClaimGuard "just routes claims — a human adjuster always makes the decision."

## Question

Assess Cimarron Peak's compliance position under the Colorado AI Act (SB 24-205) as of the expected effective date (June 30, 2026). Specifically address: (1) For each of the three tools, whether it is a "high-risk artificial intelligence system," applying each relevant exclusion; (2) Whether ClaimGuard is a "substantial factor" given the adjuster override; (3) Whether a policy certified to ISO/IEC 27001 satisfies the risk management requirement; (4) Whether the impact assessment, consumer notice, data correction, and appeal measures are sufficient; (5) What obligations apply and by when. Use the F-I-R-A-C-O structure.
