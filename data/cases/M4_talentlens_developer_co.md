# M4 — TalentLens Developer Duties

## Metadata
- jurisdiction: CO
- doctrinal_area: developer status, developer documentation duties, discovery of algorithmic discrimination
- statutes: CO AIA (SB 24-205) §6-1-1701 through §6-1-1707
- tier: medium
- role: training
- parties: HireLattice, TalentLens

## Facts

HireLattice Inc. is a software company in Boulder, Colorado, with 85 employees. It sells "TalentLens," a model that scores job applicants' résumés and online assessments. HireLattice built TalentLens by taking a general-purpose foundation model licensed from a third party and fine-tuning it on two million historical hiring records. Forty Colorado employers use TalentLens scores to decide which applicants receive an interview.

HireLattice gives each customer a two-page "model card" that describes the model's architecture and its accuracy on benchmark tests. It provides nothing else. In particular it provides no description of known limitations relating to discrimination, no summary of the training data or of known biases in that data, no statement of intended or inappropriate uses, and no guidance on how a person should use or monitor the scores. When customers have asked for information to complete their own impact assessments, HireLattice has refused, citing trade secrets. HireLattice's website does not list TalentLens or describe how its risks are managed.

In December 2025, HireLattice's internal testing found that TalentLens scores applicants older than 55 materially lower than younger applicants with the same qualifications. Engineers changed the model in January 2026 and believe the gap is closed. HireLattice did not tell its customers, the applicants who had been scored, or any regulator.

HireLattice's chief executive takes two positions. First: "We only fine-tuned someone else's model, so we are not the developer." Second: "Employers decide how to use the scores, so compliance is their responsibility, not ours." He also believes that because the company found and fixed the problem itself, it is automatically protected from enforcement.

## Question

Assess HireLattice's position under the Colorado AI Act (SB 24-205) as of the expected effective date (June 30, 2026). Specifically address: (1) Whether HireLattice is a "developer," given that it fine-tuned a third party's model; (2) Each documentation duty owed to deployers and whether the model card satisfies it; (3) The public statement requirement; (4) What the December 2025 finding requires HireLattice to do, to whom, and within what time; (5) Whether the rebuttable presumption of reasonable care is available on these facts, and what obligations HireLattice must now undertake. Use the F-I-R-A-C-O structure.
