# M8 — PremiumPath Discovery and Cure

## Metadata
- jurisdiction: CO
- doctrinal_area: discovery of algorithmic discrimination, notice duty, cure and rebuttable presumption
- statutes: CO AIA (SB 24-205) §6-1-1701 through §6-1-1707
- tier: medium
- role: test
- parties: Clearwater Auto Insurance, PremiumPath

## Facts

Clearwater Auto Insurance is based in Lakewood, Colorado, and has 240 full-time employees. Its data science team built "PremiumPath," a model that sets auto insurance premiums for Colorado policyholders. Clearwater trained PremiumPath on its own policy and claims data. Premiums are set automatically, with no human review of individual quotes.

On March 2, 2026, an internal review found that PremiumPath charged materially higher premiums to policyholders who had chosen Spanish as their preferred language, after controlling for driving record and vehicle. The cause was a "preferred language" field that the model had learned to use. Engineers removed the field and released a corrected model on April 6, 2026.

About 3,100 policyholders are still paying premiums that were set by the earlier model. Their current policy terms run past June 30, 2026, and Clearwater does not plan to re-rate them before renewal.

Clearwater has not told the Attorney General or any policyholder about the finding. Its general counsel advises against doing so, on three grounds: the problem "was found and fixed before the law takes effect"; language preference "is not a protected characteristic"; and, having cured the problem itself, Clearwater "will have a presumption of reasonable care in any case."

Clearwater has a risk management program aligned with the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, and it completed an impact assessment of PremiumPath in January 2026. It gives policyholders no notice that an AI system sets premiums and offers no way to correct data or to appeal a premium.

## Question

Assess Clearwater's position under the Colorado AI Act (SB 24-205) as of the expected effective date (June 30, 2026). Specifically address: (1) Whether the March 2026 finding is "algorithmic discrimination" under the statutory definition; (2) What notice duties arise from the discovery, to whom, and by when, taking into account that 3,100 policyholders will still be paying the affected premiums after the effective date; (3) Whether each element of the rebuttable presumption of reasonable care is met; (4) Who can enforce the Act against Clearwater and what the exposure is; (5) Clearwater's role or roles under the Act and the obligations it must undertake. Use the F-I-R-A-C-O structure.
